What is hydration?

Hydration is how much water is in your dough compared to the flour, as a percent. Flour is always 100%.

Formula: water ÷ flour × 100.
Example: 320 g water ÷ 500 g flour = 0.64, so 64% hydration.

Counting the starter: a normal starter is half flour, half water. Add half of the starter to the flour and half to the water, then do the same math. The "effective" hydration is a bit higher.

  • 55-65%: stiffer, easy to handle
  • 68-72%: good for beginners
  • 75-80%: sticky, bigger holes, harder to shape

More water means stickier dough and a more open crumb.

Baker's percentages

Bakers describe a recipe by comparing every ingredient to the flour. Flour is 100%.

Your loaf: 500 g flour = 100%, 320 g water = 64%, 125 g starter = 25%, 8 g salt = 1.6%.

This is why you can double or halve any recipe and keep the same dough. The My Numbers tab shows these for you.

Bake day guide

Stretch & folds

Wet your hand, grab one edge of the dough, stretch it up and fold it over the middle. Turn the bowl and repeat 3 or 4 times around. Do this every 30 minutes at the start. It builds strength without kneading.

How do I know the dough is ready to shape?
  • It has grown about 1.5 to 2 times its size.
  • The top is domed and has some bubbles.
  • It jiggles when you shake the bowl.
  • Poke test: flour a finger and poke it. If the dent springs back slowly and not all the way, it's ready.

Warm kitchens go faster. Cool kitchens take longer. Around 74 to 78°F, bulk often takes about 3.5 to 4 hours total.

Cold proof (the fridge rest)

The shaped loaf rests in the fridge, usually 12 to 24 hours. Longer gives a tangier flavor. Past about 48 hours the dough can start to break down. The cold also makes the dough easier to score.

Baking

Use a preheated Dutch oven, about 5 to 6 quarts for a loaf this size. A common method is a hot start for about 20 minutes with the lid on to trap steam, then 20 to 25 minutes with the lid off at around 450°F. Ovens differ, so go by color: deep golden brown.

Let the loaf cool completely before slicing. Cutting hot bread can make the inside gummy.

Starter tips

A healthy starter doubles in about 4 to 8 hours after a feed and has a domed top with bubbles. Use it at its peak. A 1:1:1 feed means equal weights of starter, flour and water.

Troubleshooting

Dense, tight crumb

Usually under-fermented. Let the dough rise longer on the counter, and check that your starter is really active (doubled) before mixing.

Flat loaf

Often a weak starter, over-proofing or rushing the rise. Try the poke test: if the dent never springs back, it's over-proofed. If it springs back right away, it needs more time.

Gummy inside

Cut too soon while hot, or over-proofed. Cool the loaf fully.

Weak oven spring

Weak gluten, a weak starter or too little water. Make sure the stretch and folds are done and the dough is not stiff.

These are general guidelines from common sourdough sources like King Arthur, The Perfect Loaf and Pantry Mama. Your kitchen and flour may change things, so trust your dough.
